Based on your existing documentation setup, you have a solid foundation for project management and development tracking. However, there are several key documents that would significantly benefit your solo commercial game project:

## Essential Missing Documents

**Game Design Document (GDD)** - This is arguably the most critical missing piece. A GDD serves as your project's blueprint and should outline core mechanics, gameplay systems, technical specifications, and art direction. For a solo developer, even a basic single-page GDD can reduce development time by up to 40% by keeping you focused on your core vision. Your GDD should include your game's core concept, design pillars, main features and mechanics, target platform and audience, visual style, and basic story elements.

**Technical Design Document (TDD)** - Since you're working solo, a TDD will help you maintain consistency in your technical decisions. This should outline your technology stack, development tools, software architecture, hardware requirements, and performance optimization strategies. This becomes especially important as your project grows in complexity.

**Art Bible** - Even for a solo project, documenting your visual style guidelines, color palettes, character design principles, and UI/UX standards will ensure visual consistency throughout development. This prevents you from making conflicting artistic decisions later in the project.

## Recommended Additional Documents

**Story Bible** - If your game has narrative elements, a dedicated story document separate from your GDD will help you maintain narrative consistency, track character development, and plan story progression.

**Level Design Document** - This should detail your level progression structure, difficulty curves, and specific level layouts or mechanics. It's particularly valuable for planning player progression and ensuring balanced gameplay.

**Monetization Strategy Document** - Since this is a commercial project, documenting your pricing strategy, potential DLC plans, marketing approach, and revenue projections will help guide business decisions throughout development.

## Integration with Your Existing System

Your current documentation already covers project management well with daily progress reports and task tracking. The missing pieces focus more on the creative and technical vision aspects. Consider creating a simple one-page GDD first, then expanding it as needed. Link these new documents to your existing README and roadmap to maintain your organized structure.

The key is not to over-document but to focus on what will actually guide your development decisions and keep you aligned with your original vision throughout the project.
